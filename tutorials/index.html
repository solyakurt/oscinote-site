Tutorials and tips

These are the guides built into the app, where they also work offline.

Getting started

Oscinote follows one workflow: Tune → Record → Analyze → Practice → Compare → Save. Choose your instrument, start the tuner, then record a take in Record & Analyze. Everything works offline and stays on this computer.

The microphone is used only after you press Start tuner or Record. Stop releases it at once.

Choosing your instrument

Open the Instrument field on the Tuner screen. Instruments with open strings (guitars, basses, ukulele, mandolin, banjo, bowed strings) get tunings, automatic string detection and a string lock. Winds, piano, voice and Other get a chromatic tuner and an optional target note. Transposing winds can show written note names; the measured frequency is always concert pitch.

Oscinote analyses one note at a time. Chords and several instruments at once can be recorded and played back but are not transcribed.

Using the tuner

Press Start tuner and play one note. The meter shows the deviation in cents from the target; it turns green and says Tuned after staying within tolerance for 0.6 s. The speaker buttons play a reference tone; detection pauses while it sounds.

A4 calibration (415–466 Hz), tolerance and noise gate are next to the meter. If nothing is detected, check the input level: the gate mark shows how loud a note must be.

Recording a take

In Record & Analyze press Record (or R), play, then Stop. The take is saved to the open project immediately, with the instrument and tuning you had selected. A crash during recording loses at most the last second: the next start recovers the take.

Watch the level meter. Clipping (samples at full scale) and very quiet input are reported; lower or raise the input gain and record again. WAV files can be imported and every take can be exported as a 24-bit WAV.

Reading the analysis

After recording, the take is analysed on this computer: the pitch curve, detected notes, each note’s offset from the nearest equal-tempered note, its spread, vibrato rate and extent, and time the detector was not confident about (grey, dashed). Uncertain detection describes the measurement, not your playing.

Without a reference Oscinote does not know which notes you intended, so it never calls a note wrong. Compare a take with another take or a MIDI file for that. Accuracy on test tones: note medians within 3 ¢; details in “How measured” next to each value.

Looping and slowing down

In Practice set an A–B loop (drag the edges, type the times, or snap to detected notes) and choose a speed between 50 and 125 %. Slower or faster playback keeps the pitch: the loop is time-stretched with WSOLA (pitch kept within ±2 ¢ on test tones).

The tempo trainer shows the practice tempo in BPM and the resulting speed. Steps advance when you press Next step. Record a retake from the same screen; the original stays unchanged.

Exercises

The practice library holds original exercises and short public-domain melodies (sources are shown in each player), realised for your instrument inside its measured range. Follow a learning path or browse the cards, open the player, listen to the reference (with an optional A–B bar loop), record an attempt with a count-in, and read the result: which notes were found, how many were within tolerance and, for rhythm exercises, how many onsets were on the grid. The staff then colours each note by its measured offset. The rule is shown with every result.

An exercise is “completed” only when its rule is met. Completion says nothing about musicianship.

Comparing takes

Compare pairs the notes of two takes (missing and extra notes are shown as such), fits their tempos and lists per note what changed: the offset in each take, onset and duration differences, spread and vibrato. “Closer” or “further” refers only to the distance from the nearest equal-tempered note and only beyond the measurement margin; otherwise it says no measurable difference.

A MIDI file can serve as the reference. “Practise this note” opens it as a loop in Practice.

Projects and backups

Takes are kept in projects on this computer. Every change is saved immediately. In Projects you can rename, duplicate, delete (with confirmation) and export a project as one .oscinote backup file; restoring a backup always creates a new project and never overwrites one.

Progress and privacy

Progress shows time with audio running, saved takes and exercise attempts per day. It is private, can be switched off, exported as CSV or JSON and cleared. Achievements are optional and mark things you did in the app, never your ability. Suggestions are computed on this computer and always show the measurement behind them.

Troubleshooting

No sound detected: check that the right input is selected (Settings → Audio) and that the input level moves. On Windows, allow microphone access for desktop apps in Settings → Privacy & security → Microphone; on Linux, check the input device in your system’s sound settings.

The tuner says “No audio is arriving”: the system paused the input; stop and start again.

What Oscinote does not do

It does not transcribe chords or several instruments at once, does not recognise chords, does not grade musical quality, does not guess the melody you intended, and is not a multitrack recorder or DAW. Accuracy figures come from synthetic test signals; real instruments read less steadily, and no physical-instrument accuracy is claimed beyond what is documented.
